not rewordings.

```

```

Optimises for a CIO or CDO reading rather than a keyword filter. Says what you fix,
not what you are called. Weaker in search, stronger in a warm introduction. Choose


Use only if you are comfortable answering that immediately.

**Do not** use a headline listing more than about four concepts. Keyword soup reads
as unfocused, which is the exact perception the positioning is meant to fix.

## 3. Experience wording

**Do not rewrite history.** Dates, employers and titles stay exactly as they are.
What changes is what each bullet emphasises.

### The rule

Every role should answer three things in its first two lines: **scope** (what you
owned), **architecture** (what you designed), **outcome** (what changed). Most
LinkedIn experience sections answer only the first.

### Short recent engagements

Two recent entries are consultancies and one is a career break. The Phase 0 audit
flags this as a job-hopping perception risk. The correction is framing, not
concealment:

- Where accurate, describe the work as an **enterprise AI transformation
  engagement** or **consulting assignment** in the first line. A reader who sees
  "engagement" stops reading it as a job that ended early.
- The **career break** should stay visible and be labelled plainly. An unexplained
  gap invites worse assumptions than a stated one.

### Title consistency — open issue

Your portfolio and your resume currently disagree on three employers' titles
(ZainTECH, ADQ/Next50, Bosch). Both documents are public. **LinkedIn is the third
surface, and it decides which of the two looks like the error.** Resolve this before
any authority push. You chose the site's titles as authoritative; LinkedIn and the
resume should follow.
